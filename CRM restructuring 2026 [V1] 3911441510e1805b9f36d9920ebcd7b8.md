# CRM restructuring 2026 [V1]

## CRM rules — V1 operating structure

This page defines the **V1 operating structure** for the ReFi Barcelona CRM. The proposal is to start using this structure as the default way of working, unless there are clear objections, and then evolve it over time based on real use.

The goal of V1 is not to freeze the CRM forever. It is to establish a stable, coherent structure that humans and agents can use consistently now. After some weeks or months of use, feedback and changes can be bundled into a future **V2**.

The CRM should work as:

- an internal relationship database for ReFi Barcelona;
- a clean registry of people, organizations, spaces, networks, funders, and public bodies;
- a basis for operational workflows such as outreach, collaborations, newsletter imports, and ecosystem mapping;
- a future source for publishable ecosystem views, such as regenerative economy maps or CATBIS interfaces;
- a database that remains readable, exportable, and portable beyond Notion.

For that reason, the structure should stay simple, legible, and interoperable. Properties should be useful both to humans scanning the database and to agents or other systems that may enrich, query, or migrate the data later.

## Core principle

### One row = one actor

Each CRM row should represent one actor/entity.

This means:

- one person = one row;
- one organization = one row;
- one public body = one row;
- one network/ecosystem = one row;
- one space = one row;
- one funder = one row.

Avoid mixing a person and an organization in the same row. If a person is the relevant contact for an organization, create/use the person’s own row and connect it through `Contact person`.

## View logic

### 1. Basic-Orgs + Basic-Inds — canonical actor registry

**Purpose:** These are the basic maintenance views for keeping the CRM clean as a general actor database.

**Basic-Orgs** is for organizations and collective actors. It is filtered by `Agency` containing `org`.

Use it for:

- organizations;
- associations;
- cooperatives;
- companies;
- foundations;
- public bodies;
- spaces;
- networks/ecosystems;
- funders.

**Basic-Inds** is for individual people. It is filtered by `Agency` containing `ind`.

Use it for:

- personal contacts;
- collaborators;
- founders / team members linked to organizations;
- event contacts;
- funder contacts;
- newsletter contacts when they are individual people.

**Shared logic:**

- One row = one actor/entity.
- Organizations and individuals should not be mixed in the same row.
- The `Agency` property defines the basic type of actor:
    - `org` = organization or collective actor;
    - `ind` = individual person;
    - `space` = physical or community space;
    - `public body` = public institution or public-sector actor;
    - `network/ecosystem` = network, coalition, alliance, or ecosystem-level actor;
    - `funder` = actor with a funding role.
- For organizations, keep contact details light and scannable. If there is a known person, create/use an individual CRM row and connect it via `Contact person`.
- For individuals, use `Email`, `Phone number`, `Website`/LinkedIn, and `Notes` to preserve contact context.
- `Notes` should explain relationship context: why we know the actor, who introduced them, what they are relevant for, and any uncertainty.

**Agent rule:** Basic-Orgs and Basic-Inds are the safest views for general cleanup, deduplication, and basic CRM enrichment. They should preserve the database as a general-purpose actor registry, not only as a ReFi/bioregional map.

### 2. ORSH — Operationally Relevant Stakeholders

**Meaning:** ORSH = Operationally Relevant Stakeholders.

**Purpose:** This view collects actors that are relevant to ReFi Barcelona operationally: people or organizations we collaborate with, want to collaborate with, or need to track as part of communication / relationship workflows.

**Current filter logic:** `2NDTAG` contains at least one of:

- `currently collaborating with`;
- `wanna collaborate with`;
- `mailing list`.

**How to use the ORSH tags:**

- `currently collaborating with` = actors with an active relationship, ongoing project, collaboration, or near-term coordination relevance.
- `wanna collaborate with` = actors that are strategically interesting but not yet active collaborators.
- `mailing list` = contacts imported from newsletter / mailing-list workflows. These may be individuals or organizations. They belong in the CRM because they are part of the communication graph, but they should not automatically be treated as strategic partners.
- Other future operational tags can be added to `2NDTAG` if they represent practical relationship-management states.

**Operational logic:**

- ORSH is not a thematic map. It is a working relationship layer.
- Actors in ORSH may be local, Catalan, Spanish, European, or international if they are operationally relevant.
- The reason an actor appears in ORSH should be understandable from `2NDTAG`, `Notes`, `Contact person`, `related programs and initiatives`, `Projects & Areas`, `Events`, `Funding Opportunities`, `Artifacts`, or `SAP`.
- When useful, link ORSH actors to the concrete workspace objects they are involved in: related projects/areas, events, programs/initiatives, funding opportunities, and artifacts.
- If an actor is only interesting as ecosystem intelligence but has no operational relationship, use RCSH/CATBIS tagging instead of ORSH unless there is also a collaboration reason.

**Newsletter logic:**

- Mailing-list contacts can live in the same CRM.
- When uploaded, newsletter people/contacts should receive `2NDTAG = mailing list`.
- If a mailing-list contact later becomes strategically relevant, add another `2NDTAG` such as `wanna collaborate with` or `currently collaborating with` instead of replacing `mailing list`.
- If a mailing-list contact is an individual connected to an organization, create or connect the organization row via `Contact person` where useful.

**Agent rule:** When adding an actor to ORSH, make the operational reason explicit. Do not tag an actor as `currently collaborating with` unless the current relationship is clear from the data or user instruction. If unsure, use `wanna collaborate with` and add uncertainty in `Notes`.

### 3. RCSH — Regenerative Catalan Stakeholders

**Meaning:** RCSH = Regenerative Catalan Stakeholders.

**Purpose:** This view collects actors that may matter for the **Regenerant Catalunya Map** project (working title), a Catalan regenerative ecosystem map/website we are proposing to Miceli Social to co-develop and co-launch.

This overlaps with what a future “bioregional intelligence system” might aim to achieve, but for now we keep it simple: the concrete goal is a **Regenerant Catalunya** map version to be delivered to Miceli Social in **September**. A broader bioregional intelligence system can come later (possibly building on this work).

**Current filter logic:** `2NDTAG` contains `CATBIS`.

> For now, we keep the `CATBIS` tag, but it refers to the **Regenerant Catalunya Map project** (not a separate bioregional intelligence system).
> 

**Scope for now:** RCSH includes actors working toward, enabling, funding, researching, or organizing around:

- bioregional regeneration in Catalunya;
- regenerative finance;
- technology / web3 / data infrastructure for regeneration;
- bioeconomy;
- circular economy;
- landscape finance;
- regenerative agriculture and food systems;
- ecological restoration, biodiversity, and territorial resilience;
- Catalan ecosystem networks, clusters, and institutional actors relevant to this field.

The boundaries of RCSH are expected to evolve.

For now, we just include in this view entities that are either located in Catalunya, or entities that have network/ecosystem operations which concretely impacts Catalunya (for example, network with x nodes and one of this nodes is in Catalunya). Interesting regenerative / around regeneration projects that are not in Catalunya and don’t entangle with any project or area in Catalunya, are not to be included here.

### Memes — current set + meaning

The 4 memes available now (`Regeneration`, `Bioeconomy`, `Bioregionalism`, `Impact Investing`): entities can have 1, 2, 3, or 4 of them depending on what they do. This is mostly a self-narrative element: these memes exist as zones of meaning in society and some actors identify with one or more of them in their own ways (but they also refer to concrete practices / approaches). See the specific rules for each meme because, at the moment, each of them has a specific tag function.

#### Regeneration

This is the basic meme in common across almost all entities in the DB, whether they use it explicitly or not. Differently than for the other memes, we’ll mark all entities here with this because we have a very broad, operational definition of regeneration. In the notes we’ll mark in some way the entities that are actually using this meme explicitly and substantially (TBD).

This is the **whole transition field**.

It answers:

> Who is relevant to the social, ecological, economic, cultural, political, and financial transition of Catalunya toward regenerative futures?
> 

It includes:

- agroecology
- water commons
- energy democracy
- cooperative economy
- urban commons
- climate justice
- digital commons
- public policy
- education
- regenerative finance
- bioregional design
- movement infrastructure
- food systems
- forests
- biomaterials
- circular economy

#### Bioeconomy

This is one **material-economic subsystem** inside the broader field of regeneration.

It answers:

> Who is working on biological resources, bio-based production, and circular/regenerative value chains in Catalunya?
> 

It includes:

- forestry
- biomass
- timber
- agri-food
- fisheries
- biomaterials
- bioplastics
- organic residues
- bioenergy
- regenerative agriculture as production economy
- circular bio-based industry
- bioeconomy research, funding, and cluster infrastructure

#### Bioregionalism

For this, it’s not super relevant. We’ll just tag with this tag entities / programs & initiatives which use this specific word in their work, and that are promoting bioregionalism explicitly.

#### Impact Investing

With this, we want to tag all the financial entities, funders and programs/initiatives that are not traditional and/or extractive finance (coop finance, impact investing, regenerative finance / investors, ethical banks, etc). Maybe at some point we’ll evolve this with sub-categories, but for now let’s leave it like this 

**How to fill RCSH rows:**

- Add `2NDTAG = CATBIS`.
- Use `Description` for a short human-readable summary.
- Use `SAP` for the deeper strategic analysis / agent-readable synthesis when available.
- Use `Memes` for broad narrative/thematic categories such as `Regeneration`, `Bioregionalism`, `Impact Investing`, `Eco Social Transition`, and `Bioeconomy`.
- Use `Area1` to mark geographic scope:
    - `catalan`;
    - `international`;
    - `Ops in Spain`;
    - `Ops in Catalunya`;
    - `Ops in Europe`.
- Use `Area2` for Catalan/regional territorial specificity when known, such as comarca, landscape, climate zone, or broader bioregional area. Use [Catalunya — Comarques, Provinces & Climate Zones](https://app.notion.com/p/Catalunya-Comarques-Provinces-Climate-Zones-26d1441510e18278923b01a631437342?pvs=21) as the reference page for this tagging logic.
- Use `Ontology` / `Ontology Tags` for more precise thematic classification when the ontology is mature enough.
- Use `related programs and initiatives` when the actor participates in, organizes, funds, or is linked to a specific program/initiative.
- When useful, link the actor to related `Projects & Areas`, `Events`, `Funding Opportunities`, and `Artifacts` so CATBIS/RCSH entries can be understood through the concrete ecosystem objects they connect to.
- Use `Notes` for uncertainty, review flags, and why the actor may belong in the Regenerant Catalunya map scope.

**RCSH vs ORSH:**

- ORSH answers: “Who matters for ReFi BCN operations and relationships?”
- RCSH answers: “Who may matter for the Regenerant Catalunya Map?”
- An actor can be in both if they are both operationally relevant and map-relevant.
- Example: an actor with `2NDTAG = currently collaborating with` and `CATBIS` appears in both views.
- Example: an actor with `2NDTAG = mailing list` appears in ORSH but should only appear in RCSH if they are also tagged `CATBIS`.

**Agent rule:** When adding to RCSH, do not assume that every regenerative or international actor belongs. Prefer actors with a Catalan territorial link, Catalunya operations, ecosystem infrastructure relevance, or strong relevance to the map scope. If the fit is uncertain, keep the row but write the uncertainty in `Notes`.

## Programs & Initiatives layer for the Regenerant Catalunya Map

The Regenerant Catalunya Map will feed from both:

- the CRM / RCSH actor layer, using `2NDTAG = CATBIS`; and
- the [Programs & Initiatives](https://app.notion.com/p/4da8003266a34efd96321ac616d6c5a4?pvs=21) database, using `2NDTAG = CATBIS`.

This means the map should distinguish between **actors/entities** and **programs/initiatives**.

### Actor vs program / initiative

Use the CRM when the row is an actor/entity:

- a legal organization;
- an association, cooperative, company, foundation, public body, funder, space, network, or ecosystem actor;
- an individual person;
- an entity that can be contacted, partnered with, governed, funded, or related to other entities.

Use the Programs & Initiatives database when the row is a program/initiative:

- a project, campaign, funding round, accelerator, research project, public program, policy process, platform initiative, learning program, call, or named workstream;
- something organized by one or more entities but not itself primarily the legal actor;
- a time-bound or purpose-bound effort with activities, outputs, participants, reports, events, or outcomes.

**Rule of thumb:** if the thing has a legal entity, it is an organization and belongs in the CRM. If it is a named program run by that entity, the program belongs in Programs & Initiatives and should be related back to the entity.

If something is both an entity and an initiative, create two entries only when both meanings are operationally useful:

- one CRM row for the legal / organizational actor;
- one Programs & Initiatives row for the specific initiative / program;
- connect them through `related entities`.

These dual cases should be rare and deliberate.

### 2NDTAG

**What it is:** `2NDTAG` in Programs & Initiatives is the same second-layer CATBIS marker used in the CRM, applied to program/initiative rows for the Regenerant Catalunya Map layer.

Use `2NDTAG = CATBIS` when a program or initiative is relevant to the Regenerant Catalunya Map.

The inclusion criteria are similar to RCSH, but applied to programs/initiatives rather than actors. A program/initiative can be CATBIS-relevant if it:

- operates in Catalunya;
- has concrete Catalunya participants, pilots, territories, partners, or institutional links;
- supports Catalan regeneration, bioeconomy, food systems, water, energy, circular economy, ecological restoration, social economy, climate adaptation, rural development, or territorial resilience;
- produces knowledge, funding, infrastructure, coordination, or public-policy processes useful for the map.

Do not tag a program/initiative as `CATBIS` only because it is generically regenerative, international, inspirational, or Iberian. There must be a concrete Catalunya link or a strong map-specific reason.

### Moving misplaced CRM rows

During CRM curation, if a CRM row is actually a program or initiative rather than an actor/entity:

- move or recreate it in Programs & Initiatives;
- assign `2NDTAG = CATBIS` if it meets the program/initiative inclusion criteria;
- relate it to the relevant CRM entity or entities through `related entities` when possible;
- remove or archive the CRM row only when it is clearly not an actor/entity and there is no need to preserve it as a CRM actor.

If the item has both a legal entity and a named initiative, keep the CRM entity and create a separate Programs & Initiatives row only if the initiative itself needs to be tracked.

**Example:** Aliança contra la Pobresa Energètica is best treated primarily as an entity/network actor in the CRM, because it is a movement/alliance with organizational identity and ongoing actor-like role. If there is a specific campaign, legal initiative, report process, or program run by APE, that specific effort can separately live in Programs & Initiatives and be related back to the APE CRM row.

## Area2 tagging reference for agents

[Catalunya — Comarques, Provinces & Climate Zones](https://app.notion.com/p/Catalunya-Comarques-Provinces-Climate-Zones-26d1441510e18278923b01a631437342?pvs=21)

The Area2 tag system should help agents describe **where in Catalunya an actor is territorially relevant**, beyond the broad `Area1` scope. For CATBIS/RCSH rows, Area2 should be filled when there is enough evidence from the actor’s website, address, project geography, or public activity.

**How agents should populate `Area2`:**

- Use [Catalunya — Comarques, Provinces & Climate Zones](https://app.notion.com/p/Catalunya-Comarques-Provinces-Climate-Zones-26d1441510e18278923b01a631437342?pvs=21) as the knowledge reference for Area2 tagging. It defines the comarca list, province context, and climate-zone groupings agents should use when assigning tags.
- Prefer the most specific reliable territorial unit available, with **comarca as the maximum resolution**. Do not go more granular than comarca level in `Area2`.
- Use the actor’s actual operational geography, not only its legal address. A headquarters address is not enough if the work clearly happens elsewhere.
- If the actor is clearly rooted in one comarca, use that comarca tag, e.g. `Garrotxa`, `Baix Ebre`, `Bages`, `Segrià`.
- If the actor works across several specific comarques, add all relevant comarca tags when there is evidence.
- If the actor works across a known climate area or territorial sub-area, add the relevant climate-area / sub-area tag where available, and add the underlying comarca tags when the comarques are known.
- If the actor works at Catalunya-wide scale without a more specific territorial focus, use `regional`.
- If the actor works in Catalunya as a consultant, service provider, platform, funder, network, research actor, or institutional intermediary without a clear local/sub-regional territorial focus, use `regional` rather than guessing comarques.
- If such a Catalunya-wide actor is focused on a specific sub-area, climate area, landscape, watershed, mountain range, coastal area, delta, or set of comarques, use the corresponding Area2 tags instead of only `regional`.
- If the actor works at province scale, use the province as context in Notes, but prefer comarca-level Area2 tags when the relevant comarques are known.
- If the actor is linked to a landscape, watershed, mountain range, coastline, delta, rural/urban bioregion, or climate-sensitive geography, use the reference page to translate that geography into likely comarca and climate-zone relevance.
- If the territorial fit is uncertain, leave `Area2` empty or use the best conservative tag, and explain the uncertainty in `Notes` or `Review flags`.

**Climate-zone logic:**

- Climate zones should not replace territory tags, but they help agents infer ecological context.
- For actors working on food systems, water, biodiversity, restoration, resilience, land use, rural development, or climate adaptation, agents can use the climate-zone classification as supporting context for the `SAP`, `Notes`, or future ecological tags.
- Example: an actor in `Garrigues`, `Segrià`, or `Pla d'Urgell` may be relevant to semi-arid / steppe dynamics; an actor in `Garrotxa` or `Gironès` may be relevant to humid subtropical / wet inland systems; an actor in `Aran`, `Pallars Sobirà`, or `Alta Ribagorça` may be relevant to mountain or Atlantic/Pyrenean systems.
- Do not infer a climate-sensitive role only from location. The actor’s mission or activities must also support that interpretation.

**Agent rule:** `Area2` is a territorial specificity field, not a generic topic field. Fill it only when the actor has a clear Catalan territorial footprint. Use the linked Catalunya reference page to normalize comarca/province/climate-zone understanding, and use `Notes` for uncertainty.

## Shared V1 properties

### Name

**What it is:** The actor/entity name.

**How to fill:**

- Use the most commonly used public name.
- For organizations, use the organization name as written on their website.
- For individuals, use first + last name where possible.
- Avoid long qualifiers in the title; put them in `Notes`.

**Good examples:** `Coop57`, `AETHNIC`, `Maria Garcia`.

### Website

**What it is:** Main website / canonical URL.

**How to fill:**

- Prefer the homepage domain unless a specific subpage is the real entry point.
- Use one URL only.
- If there are multiple relevant links, put the rest in `Notes` or the page body if the actor deserves a richer dossier.
- If the actor has no website, leave empty and use `Notes` or LinkedIn where appropriate.

### Description

**What it is:** A short summary to understand who/what the actor is at a glance.

**How to fill:**

- Use 1–2 clear sentences.
- Prefer describing what they do + where they operate.
- If Hermes/autofill proposes a description, sanity-check it and adjust if it is too long, too generic, or inaccurate.

### Agency

**What it is:** The basic actor-type taxonomy.

**How to fill:**

- Individuals: `ind`.
- Organizations: always include `org`.
- Add additional tags where appropriate:
    - `space`;
    - `public body`;
    - `network/ecosystem`;
    - `funder`.

If unsure between categories, choose the best current guess and write the uncertainty in `Notes`.

### 2NDTAG

**What it is:** A second-layer tag field for operational and special-purpose CRM views.

Current important values:

- `currently collaborating with`;
- `wanna collaborate with`;
- `mailing list`;
- `CATBIS`;
- `bioreg`.

Use `2NDTAG` when a row should enter a purpose-specific layer such as ORSH or RCSH. Do not use it as a replacement for `Agency`, `Memes`, or `Ontology`.

### 3RDTAG

- `Ecosystems`;
- `Food`;
- `Energy`;
- `Water`;
- `Circular`;
- `Social`.

An entity can have more than one `3RDTAG`. For example, an agroecology cooperative working on community food systems and soil restoration may have `Food`, `Social`, and `Ecosystems`.

`3RDTAG` is independent from the previous tagging system. It does not replace `Memes`, `Ontology`, `Ontology Tags`, `Area1`, or `Area2`; it is a simple cross-cutting map/category layer for CATBIS entities.

### Memes

**What it is:** Broad thematic / narrative categories.

Current examples:

- `Regeneration`;
- `Bioregionalism`;
- `Impact Investing`;
- `Eco Social Transition`;
- `Bioeconomy`.

Use `Memes` for broad orientation, especially in RCSH/CATBIS work. Prefer a few strong tags over many weak ones.

#### Bioeconomy meme — current compiling rule

For now, use `Memes = Bioeconomy` for **CATBIS/RCSH entities that belong to the bioeconomy categories defined in the Catalunya bioeconomy research map**.

Working definition:

> Bioeconomy in Catalunya refers to the subset of CATBIS/RCSH concerned with biological resources, bio-based production, circular/regenerative value chains, and the institutions, companies, networks, funders, and knowledge systems that support them.
> 

Tag a CATBIS actor with `Bioeconomy` when it is substantially connected to one or more of these categories:

- **Agri-food systems:** production, distribution, processing, residues, supply chains, food sovereignty, agroecology, regenerative agriculture when connected to productive/economic systems.
- **Forestry and forest bioeconomy:** timber, biomass, forest management, forest science, forest-based value chains, proximity materials, forest resilience, and bioenergy from forest resources.
- **Biomaterials and bio-based industry:** biomaterials, bioplastics, bio-based polymers, natural construction materials, circular materials, industrial biotechnology, and material innovation based on biological resources.
- **Circular bioeconomy:** organic-residue valorization, industrial symbiosis involving biological/organic flows, composting, biomass loops, circular agri-food/forestry systems, and waste-to-resource models.
- **Blue bioeconomy:** fisheries, marine resources, coastal food systems, responsible seafood, marine biodiversity, and maritime bio-based value chains.
- **Bioenergy:** energy from biomass, biogas, agricultural residues, forestry residues, or other biological resources. Do not tag general renewable-energy actors as `Bioeconomy` unless this bio-based energy link is clear.
- **Rural and territorial bioeconomy infrastructure:** rural development networks, Local Action Groups, agricultural/cooperative federations, territorial innovation hubs, or public bodies directly supporting bio-based value chains.
- **Knowledge, policy, cluster, and funding infrastructure for bioeconomy:** research centres, professional associations, cluster organizations, public agencies, reports, funders, or investors specifically connected to agri-food, forestry, biomaterials, biomass, blue bioeconomy, or circular bio-based sectors.

Do **not** use `Bioeconomy` merely because an actor is broadly regenerative, ecological, cooperative, or part of the ecosocial transition. Keep those actors in CATBIS/RCSH without the Bioeconomy meme unless there is a clear connection to biological resources or bio-based value chains.

Examples of CATBIS actors that may be RCSH-relevant but should not automatically receive `Bioeconomy`:

- water commons actors, unless linked to watershed-based productive bioeconomy;
- energy democracy actors, unless linked to biomass/biogas/bioenergy;
- housing, urban commons, or civic-tech actors, unless linked to bio-based construction/materials or organic/circular flows;
- general cooperative/ESS infrastructure with no specific agri-food, forestry, biomaterials, or biological-resource role;
- general regenerative finance or impact investing actors, unless they fund bioeconomy sectors;
- methodology, education, or bioregional field-building actors, unless they directly support land-based production, agroecological enterprise, or bio-based value chains.

If the actor seems potentially relevant but the bioeconomy link is unclear, keep `CATBIS`, leave `Bioeconomy` off for now, and add the uncertainty in `Notes` or `Review flags`. Later we may add a more specific “bioeconomy-adjacent / review” mechanism.

Other meme definitions still need to be specified later.

### Ontology / Ontology Tags

**What it is:** More precise thematic/sector classification.

The `Ontology` relation should connect to the Ontology database when possible. `Ontology Tags` can preserve text-based ontology tags where needed.

Use ontology fields mostly for relevant actors and ecosystem-intelligence work. Later, the ideal workflow is:

- an agent looks at the entry’s website or public information;
- checks the [Ontology](https://app.notion.com/p/3331441510e180d19eccdad5b09ee6de?pvs=21) database;
- proposes or fills the most fitting ontology tags.

### Notes

**What it is:** The catch-all field for context that should remain visible but does not yet need its own structured property.

Use `Notes` for:

- relationship details;
- founder/contact/collaborator context;
- source/context for contact information;
- uncertainty or review flags;
- why the actor matters;
- extra links;
- information that helps future CRM filling.

Avoid using `Notes` as a substitute for properties that clearly need to be filtered, sorted, exported, or published later.

### Contact person

**What it is:** A relation to individual CRM rows. It is not a Notion People property.

**How to fill:**

- If the row is an organization, link to the relevant individual row(s).
- If the individual does not exist yet, create an individual row and relate it to the organization.
- If there are multiple contacts, link the primary one and list others in `Notes` for now.
- If unknown, leave empty and write any clue or possible source in `Notes`.
- Email/phone/contact context should live in the related individual row or in `Notes`.

### related programs and initiatives

**What it is:** A relation to the Programs & Initiatives database.

Use this when an actor participates in, organizes, funds, supports, or is otherwise linked to a program/initiative.

Examples:

- an organization related to a program/initiative → represent this through the dedicated Programs & Initiatives database and clarify the role in `Notes` if needed;
- a person related to an initiative → use the relevant initiative relation and clarify whether they attended, hosted, spoke, facilitated, funded, etc.

## Pages vs Notes

By default, CRM entries should not rely on the page body. Most entries should be understandable from properties + `Notes`.

Use the page body only for actors that deserve a richer dossier, such as:

- key partners;
- strategic funders;
- complex organizations;
- actors with substantial research or relationship history;
- actors that need long-form context for agents or humans.

Working rule:

- ordinary actor → properties + `Notes`;
- relevant actor → properties + `Notes` + optional page body/dossier.

## Relationship logic

For V1, relationships should stay simple.

Use:

- `Contact person` for person ↔ organization contact relationships;
- `related programs and initiatives` for links to programs/initiatives;
- `Projects & Areas` for links to active work streams, areas, partnerships, or projects;
- `Events` for events the actor organizes, attends, hosts, sponsors, speaks at, or is otherwise connected to;
- `Funding Opportunities` for grants, calls, or funding programs connected to the actor;
- `Artifacts` for reports, sources, meeting notes, datasets, or other knowledge/information objects connected to the actor;
- `Notes` to clarify the exact meaning of a relationship.

For Basic-Orgs and Basic-Inds, the priority is still a clean actor registry. Do not over-link simple entries unless the relationship is useful. For ORSH and RCSH, linking to related projects, programs, events, funding opportunities, and artifacts is encouraged whenever it clarifies why the actor matters.

For now, avoid creating too many separate relation properties such as “formal affiliation,” “collaboration,” “partner,” “participant,” etc. If the distinction matters, write it in `Notes` or in the related program/event/artifact record.

This keeps the CRM lighter while still allowing it to represent the ecosystem network.

## Automation and enrichment logic

When adding something into the CRM Telegram topic, ideally with a URL, Hermes or another agent should be able to:

- fetch the website;
- fill the basic properties;
- write or improve the `Description`;
- propose relevant `Agency`, `Memes`, `Area1`, `Area2`, `2NDTAG`, and ontology values;
- preserve uncertainty in `Notes`.

Agents should optimize for accuracy, simplicity, and future portability. The CRM should remain readable/interactable by humans, agents, Org OS-like systems, and future non-Notion infrastructure.

## V1 → V2 evolution

This V1 should now be used as the working structure.

Instead of changing the CRM structure constantly, collect issues and improvement ideas as they emerge from real use. Later, merge the feedback into a V2.

Possible V2 topics:

- whether to add a specific “relevant actor / dossier needed” tag;
- whether commercial pipeline / offering workflows should live in the CRM or a separate database;
- how newsletter contacts should be imported, deduplicated, and segmented;
- how the CRM relates to the current ReFi BCN Knowledge Base / public map;
- whether ontology tagging should become more automated or more strictly curated;
- whether CATBIS/RCSH needs more precise inclusion criteria.
